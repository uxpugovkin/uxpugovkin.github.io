Set shifts for a slice

When an enterprise client wants to inspect retail shelves across the whole country, and I must set tasks in a plain table, I want slightly different tasks to appear automatically for each geo slice (state, city, district). This way, I avoid most of the manual work.

As a solution, I designed a calendar with easily configurable nested nodes. A manager can pick a geo slice, such as a city district, to create a specific schedule and assign workers in bulk.

The first alternative was a visual map, but it requires effort to map zones and is hard to scale and support. The second was an auto algorithm, but it is overkill for irregular activities and beyond team resources.

As a result, I removed Excel imports and exports and the manual work with a plain shop list. Task time dropped from 6–7 hours to an estimated 45 minutes.

Configurable hierarchy

Levels let a project match any geography, so a client can use regions, districts and neighborhoods, or skip levels entirely and work with plain locations. Each level is a slice of the business, and the structure is built once per project instead of being hardcoded into the product. A manager sets rules on a slice and they apply to everything nested inside it, so a difficult area can get higher pay or different limits in one action.
