Hard part

Assigning shifts isn’t just about places — it’s about people, and every self-employed worker has permissions scoped to a specific level. One worker may only be cleared to work in a single district of Samara; another across the whole city. So the location hierarchy and the permission scopes are two different structures that don’t line up neatly.

The solution was a configurable base level. Rather than hard-coding one level for everyone, the client picks the level of the hierarchy where workers get assigned: a business organized around districts sets the base at district; one that thinks in cities sets it there. Assignments then happen at that level, in bulk, so you never have to assign worker-by-shop.

The trade-off

Making the base level a setting meant trading a small amount of upfront simplicity — someone has to make that configuration decision — for a system that adapts to very different business structures instead of forcing them all into one shape. It also keeps assignment fast: you act on a whole level at once rather than shop by shop, at the cost of the fine-grained control you’d get assigning each shop individually.
